# 01
The main message of the form is to show the catastrophic losses suffered by Napoleon’s army during the Russian campaign of 1812–1813. The width of the army’s route represents the number of soldiers, so the narrowing route clearly shows how the army became smaller as it moved toward Moscow and during its retreat.

The visualization also combines the army’s movement with geographical locations, dates, and temperature data. This allows the viewer to see not only where the army traveled, but also how its size changed throughout the campaign. Overall, the figure shows how Napoleon’s Russian campaign gradually turned into a military disaster.

Kraak, M. J. (2014). _Mapping time: Illustrated by Minard’s map of Napoleon’s Russian campaign of 1812_. Esri Press.
# 02
In my opinion, the visualization does a very good job of conveying its message. The changing width of the army’s route makes the loss of soldiers easy to notice. The route becomes much narrower as the army moves toward Moscow and during the retreat, so the scale of the losses can be understood without looking at every number.

Another strength is that the visualization combines several types of information, including location, army size, direction, time, and temperature. These elements help the viewer understand the campaign as a process rather than just seeing a final number of casualties. Although the figure contains a lot of information, the main message of the army’s dramatic decline remains clear.

# 03
I think the visualization presents the data fairly overall, but it also has some limitations. Minard clearly states his data sources and even explains an assumption he made when representing some detached troops. This makes the visualization more transparent about how the data were constructed.

However, the visualization does not show the specific causes of the army’s losses, such as battle, disease, hunger, or desertion. Because the temperature data are shown together with the retreat, viewers might also assume that the cold was the main cause of the losses. Therefore, while the figure presents the decline in troop numbers effectively, it does not provide a complete explanation of why those losses occurred.
