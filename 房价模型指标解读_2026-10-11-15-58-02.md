### 🤖 Assistant
*2026/10/11 23:47:11*

Three improvement strategies are recommended. First, **Ridge regression** addresses overfitting/high variance by adding an L2 penalty that shrinks coefficients, reducing variance without eliminating features [[1]](https://scikit-learn.org/0.24/modules/generated/sklearn.linear_model.Ridge.html). Technically, it minimizes the residual sum of squares plus α||w||², controlled by regularization strength α; implement via `sklearn.linear_model.Ridge(alpha=1.0)` and tune α with `RidgeCV`. Second, **log-transforming the target** targets heteroscedasticity by compressing the scale of expensive houses, often equalizing error variance when spread grows with predicted values [[2]](https://scikit-learn.org/dev/modules/generated/sklearn.compose.TransformedTargetRegressor.html#:~:text=transformer_,-object). Use `sklearn.compose.TransformedTargetRegressor(func=np.log, inverse_func=np.exp)` wrapping any regressor. Third, **K-fold cross-validation** provides a more reliable estimate of generalization performance than a single train-test split, diagnosing whether the 0.91–0.68 gap reflects genuine overfitting or split-specific variance [[4]](http://scikit-learn.org//dev//modules/generated/sklearn.model_selection.KFold.html#sklearn.model_selection.KFold). Implement with `sklearn.model_selection.cross_val_score(estimator, X, y, cv=5)`.

### 🤖 Assistant
*2026/10/11 23:40:17*

The coefficient of determination, R², measures the proportion of variance in house prices explained by the model (James et al., 2021). The substantial drop from 0.91 in training to 0.68 in testing signals overfitting: the model has learned idiosyncrasies of the training data that fail to generalize to unseen observations (James et al., 2021). A test MSE of 28,500 indicates the average squared prediction error is roughly $28,500, meaning predictions deviate from actual sale prices by approximately $169 (√28,500) on average—substantial for typical housing transactions. Critically, the funnel-shaped residual pattern (increasing spread with higher predicted prices) violates the homoscedasticity assumption of ordinary least squares, indicating that prediction errors are systematically larger for expensive homes (James et al., 2021). This heteroscedasticity suggests the model may require transformation of the target variable or robust standard errors.

First, **Ridge regression** addresses overfitting by adding an L2 penalty that shrinks coefficients, reducing variance without eliminating features [[6]](https://scikit-learn.org/1.2/modules/generated/sklearn.linear_model.Ridge.html#sklearn.linear_model.Ridge). Technically, it minimizes the residual sum of squares plus α||w||², controlled by the regularization strength α. In Python, use `sklearn.linear_model.Ridge(alpha=1.0)` and tune α via cross-validation [[6]](https://scikit-learn.org/1.2/modules/generated/sklearn.linear_model.Ridge.html#sklearn.linear_model.Ridge).

Second, **log-transforming the target** targets heteroscedasticity by compressing the scale of expensive houses, which often equalizes error variance when spread grows with predicted values [[4]](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook//pmd/section6/pmd624.htm). Use `sklearn.compose.TransformedTargetRegressor(func=np.log, inverse_func=np.exp)` wrapping any regressor [[8]](https://scikit-learn.org/dev/modules/generated/sklearn.compose.TransformedTargetRegressor.html#:~:text=transformer_,-object).

Third, **weighted least squares (WLS)** directly addresses heteroscedasticity by assigning lower weights to high-variance observations [[2]](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/mono/10.1201/9781003162230-9/homoscedasticity-john-hoffmann?context=ubx). Estimate weights as inverse squared residuals from an initial OLS fit, then refit. This yields efficient estimates and valid standard errors [[2]](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/mono/10.1201/9781003162230-9/homoscedasticity-john-hoffmann?context=ubx).
